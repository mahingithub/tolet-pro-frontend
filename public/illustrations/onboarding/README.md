## Masters

- `design/onboarding-masters/find-home-v2.jpg`
- `design/onboarding-masters/collect-rent-v3.jpg`
- `design/onboarding-masters/shared-ledger-v3.jpg`
- `design/onboarding-masters/collect-rent-v2.jpg`, `design/onboarding-masters/shared-ledger-v2.jpg` — earlier artwork, superseded by v3

The generated illustrations were exported at 960×640, JPEG quality 85, and the `.jpg` files are kept as the masters — they are the only source the cut-outs can be regenerated from. They live outside `public/` on purpose: everything under `public/` is copied verbatim into the web bundle and, via `npm run android:sync`, into the APK, and nothing ever requests a `.jpg` at runtime. **The runtime assets are the `.png` cut-outs**, produced from the masters by `scripts/cutout-onboarding-art.mjs` — run it by hand whenever a master changes.

The masters share a white matte. That matte was the carousel's problem: it needed a stationary white surface behind the artwork, which the dark theme cannot recolour without recolouring the people, so the first screen of the app showed a white slab. The cut-outs carry real transparency instead, so the artwork sits straight on the page in either theme and the carousel has no surface of its own.

The cut is a flood of the border-connected matte only, so white that belongs to the artwork survives — the phone screens, the mug and the plant pot are enclosed by their own outlines and never touch the canvas edge. The home-search scene is the one exception: its floating listing card is untraced pure white reaching into the open margin beside the woman, so the script hands that one rectangle back explicitly. The antialiased rim is un-matted rather than clipped, which is what keeps the cut-outs free of a white halo on a dark screen.

PNG costs roughly 210 KB a scene against 130 KB for the JPEG. These ship inside the Android bundle and the welcome screen is app-only, so the cost is APK size, not a download on a Bangladeshi connection. Quantizing to 32 levels per channel — invisible on flat cartoon art, and the JPEG noise in the flat areas is what made an unquantized export three times the size — is what keeps it to that.

Intrinsic dimensions and the carousel's own slot reserve layout space; the artwork is `object-contain` and gives up height first, so a short phone shrinks the illustration rather than pushing the form under the fold. All slides load eagerly (the first at high fetch priority), avoiding a late request on the first rotation. The two v3 scenes replace paper bookkeeping with visibly hand-held smartphones. Their screens are illustrative branded feature summaries, not exact product screenshots. The original v2 files are retained as earlier artwork; only the v3 rent and shared-living scenes are used at runtime. The home-search scene remains v2.
